What this typing test measures

This test measures your typing speed and accuracy on real text over one minute. Speed is reported as net words per minute (WPM). For languages where "words per minute" does not make sense, such as Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai, it is reported as characters per minute (CPM).

Typing is a skilled motor task. It depends on how quickly you read ahead, how well you know the keyboard layout, and how smoothly your fingers overlap one keystroke with the next. A large study of 168,000 volunteers who typed 136 million keystrokes (Dhakal et al., 2018) found that fast typists are not simply faster at each key. They press keys with more overlap, called "rollover", and make fewer errors that need correcting.

How the test works

  • A passage appears in your language. Type it exactly as shown.
  • You have 60 seconds. The timer starts with your first keystroke, so you can read the first line before you begin.
  • Correct characters are highlighted as you type, and mistakes are marked.
  • Pasting is blocked. The test also checks that keystroke timing looks human, so a pasted or scripted run is refused rather than given an impossible score.

How your score is calculated

Net WPM. By long-standing convention, a "word" is 5 characters, including spaces. Your speed counts only characters typed correctly in the right position, divided by 5, per minute. This is net speed. Mistyped characters add nothing, so mashing keys cannot inflate your result.

Accuracy. The percentage of the characters you typed that were correct.

Script-aware scoring. The 5-character convention and English speed norms don't fit every writing system:

  • Chinese and Japanese are typed through an input method (pinyin or romaji, then conversion), so they are measured in characters per minute against their own scale.
  • Korean and Thai are typed directly but with many characters per word, so they are also measured in CPM on a separate scale.
  • Hindi uses WPM, but on its own scale. Conjuncts and vowel signs take several keystrokes per visible character, so the same finger speed produces fewer words.

Percentile. Your speed is placed on an approximate distribution for your script. For Latin-alphabet languages, the midpoint is about 55 WPM, in line with the roughly 52 WPM average in the Dhakal et al. data. These are approximate reference curves, not norms collected from this test.

What is a good typing speed?

  • Under 30 WPM: below average; common for people who look at the keyboard or type with two fingers
  • 30–44 WPM: slightly below average
  • 45–69 WPM: average for regular computer users
  • 70–89 WPM: above average; comfortable for most office and writing jobs
  • 90–119 WPM: superior
  • 120 WPM and above: exceptional; competitive typists sustain speeds above 150 WPM

Accuracy matters as much as speed. At 95% accuracy you are correcting about one character in twenty, and in real work that costs more time than a few extra words per minute save. Most typing jobs look for 97% accuracy or better.

How to type faster

  • Stop looking at the keyboard. Touch typing is the biggest single upgrade for most people. Speed often drops for a week or two before it rises well above where you started.
  • Accuracy first. Practise at a pace where you make few errors. Speed follows once the movements are consistent.
  • Read ahead. Fast typists look a few words past the one they are typing.
  • Practise short and often. Ten to fifteen minutes a day beats one long weekly session for building motor skills.
  • Check your setup. Relaxed wrists, a keyboard at elbow height and a comfortable key action all help. They also reduce strain over long sessions.

Research by Feit, Weir and Oulasvirta (2016) found that some fast typists use fewer than ten fingers but use them very consistently. Consistent finger-to-key mapping matters more than following the textbook method exactly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average typing speed?

Studies of general computer users put the average at roughly 40–55 WPM in English. The largest recent dataset found about 52 WPM.

What is the difference between gross and net WPM?

Gross WPM counts every character typed. Net WPM counts only the correct ones. This test reports net WPM, which is the fairer measure.

Why is my score shown in CPM?

For Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai, word length and the input process are too different for WPM to mean anything, so characters per minute is used instead.

Why was my result not scored?

Runs that are pasted, faster than a human can type, or show machine-like keystroke timing are refused, so every shared result is genuine.

Is 60 seconds long enough?

One minute gives a good snapshot of your speed. Longer tests show endurance and tend to produce slightly lower speeds.
