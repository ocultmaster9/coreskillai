Measuring Emotional Intelligence

Emotional intelligence covers how accurately you perceive emotions (in faces, tone and context), how well you understand how emotions blend and evolve, and how effectively you manage them in yourself and others. Research links higher EQ to better teamwork, leadership ratings and relationship satisfaction — effects that persist after controlling for IQ and personality.

How this test works

Scenario-based questions present realistic social situations and ask you to judge the most emotionally intelligent response, following the situational-judgment approach used in validated instruments like the MSCEIT.

Is EQ trainable? Yes, more so than IQ. Labeling emotions precisely, perspective-taking practice and reappraisal techniques show measurable gains in controlled studies.

Is self-reported EQ reliable? Self-perception often diverges from measured ability — people with lower emotional skill tend to overestimate it, which is exactly why ability-style scenario testing is more informative than asking "are you empathetic?"
