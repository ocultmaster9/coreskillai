What this personality type test measures

This free 16 personality types test gives you a four-letter code such as INTJ or ESFP, the format made famous by the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator. Each letter is one side of a pair: Extraversion or Introversion (E/I), Sensing or Intuition (S/N), Thinking or Feeling (T/F) and Judging or Perceiving (J/P). The ideas go back to Carl Jung's book Psychological Types (1921).

Unlike most free type quizzes, this one is scored from published, public-domain research items rather than invented questions, and it shows how strongly you lean toward each letter instead of only the letter itself.

How the test works

  • 48 statements from the IPIP. The items come from the Big Five Aspect Scales, part of the International Personality Item Pool, a free collection of research-grade personality items.
  • Each letter is scored from its matching Big Five trait. In a study of 468 adults, McCrae and Costa (1989) found that the four Myers-Briggs scales measure four of the Big Five traits under other names. Extraversion and Introversion line up with Big Five Extraversion, Intuition with Openness, Feeling with Agreeableness, and Judging with Conscientiousness.
  • Ten items per letter pair. About half of the items are reversed, so agreeing with everything does not push you toward one side.
  • A fifth scale, emotional reactivity. The Big Five also includes Neuroticism, how strongly a person reacts to stress, which the Myers-Briggs system leaves out. Eight items measure it and it is shown separately, without a letter.

Your percentage for each pair is your average answer on its items, placed on a scale from 0 to 100. Above 50 gives you the first letter of the pair; below 50 gives you the second.

What the letters mean

E or I: where you get your energy

Extraverted people are sociable, talkative and quick to act. Introverted people are more reserved, need time alone to recharge and often think before they speak. Introversion is not the same as shyness.

S or N: what you pay attention to

Sensing types focus on concrete facts and practical experience. Intuitive types are drawn to ideas, patterns, art and abstract problems. In Big Five terms, this pair tracks Openness to Experience.

T or F, and J or P

Thinking types weigh decisions by logic and can stay detached; Feeling types weigh the effect on people. Judging types like plans, order and closure; Perceiving types keep options open and adapt as they go. These two pairs relate to Agreeableness and Conscientiousness.

Why you get percentages, not just letters

The biggest scientific criticism of personality types is that people do not fall into two clear groups on any of these dimensions. Most people score near the middle, like height or weight, and only a few sit at the extremes. McCrae and Costa found no evidence that the Myers-Briggs scales split people into true types.

This matters because someone at 52% and someone at 48% are almost identical, yet they receive different letters. That is the main reason the same person often gets a different type when they retake a type test, a problem reviewed by Boyle (1995) and Pittenger (2005). This test therefore shows each percentage and tells you when a letter is close to the middle, so you can see how firm each part of your type is.

  • A clear preference (more than 75% or less than 25%) is less likely to change on a retest.
  • A moderate preference (60 to 75% or 25 to 40%) usually holds, but may shift.
  • A slight preference (40 to 60%) means both letters describe you about equally well.

What a type can and cannot tell you

A four-letter type is a useful shorthand for talking about how you prefer to work, decide and spend your energy. It can help you understand why a colleague plans everything while you improvise, or why a friend needs quiet after a party. It is not a measure of ability, intelligence or mental health, and no type is better than another.

Because the letters are simplified, psychologists generally do not recommend using type tests to choose employees or to predict job performance. If you want a fuller picture, the five separate trait scores of a Big Five test are more informative than one combined code.

How to get an accurate result

  • Answer as you usually are, at home and at work, not as you would like to be or as you are on an unusual day.
  • Use the middle option sparingly. If you lean even a little one way, choose that side.
  • Don't overthink each statement. Your first reaction is usually the most accurate.
  • Retake it another day if some letters are close to 50%, and look at which ones stay the same.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the official MBTI?

No. The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator is a commercial questionnaire and a trademark of The Myers-Briggs Company. This test uses the same four-letter framework but scores it from free, public-domain research items, so your type may differ from an official report.

Can my type change?

Personality is fairly stable in adults, but it does shift slowly over the years, and letters near 50% can flip from one test to the next. Treat your type as a description of your current tendencies, not a fixed label.

How is this different from the Big Five test?

Both measure the same underlying traits. The Big Five test reports five separate scores, while this test turns four of them into letters and adds a short type description. The Big Five gives more detail; the type is easier to remember and share.
