Pattern Recognition and Fluid Intelligence

Spotting the rule that governs an abstract sequence is the purest measurable form of fluid intelligence — reasoning that doesn't depend on vocabulary or schooling. Tests of this type (the best known being Raven's Progressive Matrices) are widely used across cultures precisely because they minimize language and knowledge bias.

What the test measures

Each puzzle presents visual sequences that follow hidden rules — progression, rotation, addition, symmetry or combinations. Difficulty rises as rules stack, and performance depends on systematically generating and testing hypotheses under time pressure.

Why do these puzzles feel hard? Multiple candidate rules fit the early elements; disambiguating them loads heavily on working memory. Skipping and returning often helps.

Does practice help? Familiarity with common rule types improves speed somewhat, which is why standardized tests rotate item banks. Your first unpracticed attempt is the most informative estimate of raw fluid reasoning.
